Our roots

Before soda was sweet, it was brewed.

Root beer started as a brewed root tea. Charles Hires showed his at the 1876 Centennial Exposition in Philadelphia, and druggists sold it for its medicinal qualities. Somewhere along the way the roots became flavoring and the drink became sugar. We went back to the pot.

Brewed, not flavored

Every batch is steeped from whole roots and botanicals. The ingredient list reads like a recipe.

Nothing sweet added

No cane sugar, no stevia, no monk fruit. Licorice root and vanilla do the rounding.

Made in Monkton

Small batches from Maryland horse country, sold first at the markets and taprooms down the road.

Using the syrup

A soda fountain in three steps

1

Fill a glass with ice

A tall glass works best. Chill the seltzer too, so it keeps its fizz.

2

Add 3 tablespoons of syrup

Start there and adjust. Unsweetened roots taste bolder than you expect.

3

Top with 12 oz of seltzer

Pour slowly down the side of the glass. Give it one gentle stir.

Questions

Good to know

If there’s no sugar, will it taste sweet?

It tastes like root beer without the candy. Licorice root, vanilla and cinnamon give a round, sweet-seeming flavor, but there is no sugar or sweetener of any kind. Most people find it closer to a fine sparkling water than a soda.

Isn’t sassafras banned?

Raw sassafras oil contains safrole, which the FDA removed from food in 1960. We use only safrole-free sassafras extract, which the FDA permits as a flavoring.

Is there caffeine?

No. All four sodas and syrups are caffeine-free.

How do I store the syrup?

Keep it in the refrigerator once it arrives and shake before pouring.
